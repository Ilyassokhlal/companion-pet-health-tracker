Common questions from pet owners - Why does my cat bring me dead mice or birds?
Cats hunt small prey, mainly birds and rodents such as mice. Why a cat brings its prey to its owner is poorly understood. One explanation is that cats adopt their humans into their social group and share surplus kill with them, treating the humans as at or near the top of the group's hierarchy. Another is that the cat is trying to teach its owner to hunt, or to feed the owner as it would an elderly cat or an inept kitten. That second idea does not fit the fact that male cats bring prey home too, although males take almost no part in raising kittens.

Common questions from pet owners - How much does a vet visit, consultation or appointment cost, or an operation such as spaying or neutering?
The cost of veterinary care has risen significantly, as veterinary medicine uses more and more expensive techniques and drugs. Pet insurance can pay part or all of the bill for treating an ill or injured pet, commonly reimbursing 70 to 90 percent of eligible costs after a deductible.

Common questions from pet owners - How much does it cost to spay or neuter a cat or a dog?
Spaying can be done by traditional open surgery or by laparoscopic keyhole surgery. Open surgery is more widely available, because the equipment for laparoscopic surgery is expensive. In males, a vasectomy is a more delicate procedure than castration and usually costs more.

Common questions from pet owners - Does pet insurance cover pre-existing conditions?
Most pet insurance products do not cover pre-existing conditions, and insurers may limit cover for them, which gives owners a reason to insure an animal while it is young and not yet expected to need costly care. Waiting periods often apply before cover begins. Owners usually pay the vet first and then claim the money back from the insurer.
