Common symptoms and when to call the vet
Knowing what is normal for your own animal is what makes an early problem visible. A sudden change in appearance, walking, activity, appetite, toilet habits or grooming can all be signs of illness.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Signs of illness in a dog
A dog may be unwell if it is not eating, is less active than usual, is vomiting or has diarrhoea, is urinating more or less often than normal, is coughing or sneezing, has discharge from the eyes, ears or nose, is losing hair or is very itchy or has red patches on the skin, or is limping or not putting weight on a leg. If any of these last more than a day or two, book a visit.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Signs of illness in a cat
Common signs of illness in a cat are not eating, vomiting including frequent hairballs, diarrhoea or hard dry stools, drinking and urinating more, coughing or wheezing, sneezing especially with discharge from the eyes or nose, itchy or dirty ears, hair loss or general itchiness, and stiffness, limping or not putting weight on a leg. A cat that seems unwell should be seen promptly.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Signs that cannot wait
Some signs mean an emergency rather than an appointment: trouble breathing, breathing with the mouth open, or gums that look bluish, all of which indicate a severe lack of oxygen. Pale gums, weak pulses and a racing heart are signs of shock, which is fatal if it is not treated.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Signs that cannot wait
Severe pain, severe or uncontrolled bleeding, an eye injury, a limb that cannot be used, collapse or loss of consciousness, nonstop seizures, blocked urine flow and suspected poisoning all need immediate veterinary care.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Vomiting
Occasional vomiting can have simple causes, but repeated vomiting, a tender belly, or the animal passing no stools can mean a blockage, and that needs a call to the veterinarian straight away. Vomiting is also one of the first signs of some poisonings.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Diarrhoea
Diarrhoea is on the list of signs that should be checked if it lasts more than a day or two. It is more urgent when there is blood, when the animal is also vomiting, or when it is very young, very old or already ill, because fluid loss matters more in those animals.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Not eating
Refusing food is one of the clearest signs of illness in both dogs and cats. In cats it deserves quick attention rather than watchful waiting, because cats that stop eating can develop further problems.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Drinking and urinating more
Drinking noticeably more and urinating more often are listed signs of illness in cats, and a change in how often a dog urinates is a reason to book a visit. Both can be early signs of common chronic diseases, so they are worth investigating rather than watching.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Dehydration
Signs of dehydration are skin that does not spring back when pinched, dry gums and sunken eyes. A dehydrated animal needs fluids, given by mouth or into a vein by a veterinarian.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Limping
Mild pain, swelling and limping after a knock or a twist call for rest and restricted activity, with a call to the veterinarian if the signs last more than a couple of days. A limb that cannot bear weight at all, or that looks broken, is an emergency.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Coughing and sneezing
Coughing or sneezing, especially with discharge from the eyes or nose, is a listed sign of illness in both dogs and cats. Coughing that comes with difficulty breathing, blue gums or collapse is an emergency rather than an appointment.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Itching, hair loss and skin changes
Hair loss, heavy scratching and red patches on the skin are signs to have checked. Itchy or dirty ears are on the same list for cats, and shaking the head or scratching at one ear often points to the ears rather than the skin.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Hiding and pain
Some animals hide pain, and cats in particular often withdraw rather than complain. Because pain is easy to miss, veterinarians treat for pain when a condition is known to be painful, even if the animal is not obviously showing it. A cat that hides, stops grooming or changes its routine is worth a call.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Changes in an older animal
Gradual changes in an older animal are easy to put down to age, but they are often caused by disease or dental problems that can be treated. Ageing itself is not a disease, so a change in weight, appetite, drinking, mobility or behaviour should be checked rather than accepted.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Scooting along the floor
A dog or cat that drags its bottom along the ground, strains to pass stools or licks its rear end excessively is usually telling you its anal glands are not emptying as they should. It is uncomfortable rather than an emergency, but it needs a veterinary appointment rather than home treatment.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Sudden bursts of running
Sudden frantic bursts of running in circles, known as the zoomies or as frenetic random activity periods, are normal in both dogs and cats and usually last a few minutes at most. They often come at moments of transition, such as an owner coming home, and are healthy as long as the animal has a safe space to do it in.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Snoring and noisy breathing in breeds with flat faces
Snoring, noisy or laboured breathing, gagging and fainting are typical of the airway problems that breeds with flat faces, such as pugs and bulldogs, are prone to. The same anatomy makes them overheat easily, and they should never be muzzled. Persistent noisy breathing deserves a veterinary opinion rather than being accepted as normal for the breed.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - A honking cough
A harsh, dry, hacking cough that sounds like a goose honk is the classic sign of kennel cough, which often follows contact with other dogs at kennels, shows or daycare. Coughing with breathing difficulty, blue gums or collapse is an emergency instead.

Common symptoms and when to call the vet - Drooling
Sudden heavy or frothy drooling is worth taking seriously. It appears with heat stroke, together with hot skin, rapid panting and distress. It appears with choking, together with gagging and pawing at the mouth. And it starts immediately after a dog or cat mouths a toad, together with head shaking and retching. Any of those three needs urgent care, and drooling that persists without an obvious cause deserves an appointment.
