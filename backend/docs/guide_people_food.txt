Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - How many treats and table scraps are too many?
Treats should make up no more than 10 percent of a pet's daily calories so they do not unbalance its diet, and table scraps or just giving a little bit can add up to a lot. A 10 pound dog or cat needs about 300 calories a day on average, which allows at most 30 calories of treats. An average 75 pound dog needs about 1,400 calories a day, which allows at most 140 calories of treats.
SOURCE: Treat Options for Dogs and Cats Without Unbalancing Their Diet (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2021/10/21/treat-options-for-dogs-and-cats-without-unbalancing-their-diet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Can dogs eat human food?
Treats and any other food a dog gets should not exceed 10 percent of its daily calories, and at least 90 percent should come from a complete and balanced dog food. Fruits and vegetables are good options for many dogs because they are usually low in protein, fat, sodium and phosphorus. Foods known to harm dogs, including chocolate, macadamia nuts, garlic, onions, grapes and raisins, should be avoided. Whether a particular treat suits a particular dog is worth discussing with its veterinarian, since some foods are not ideal in certain circumstances.
SOURCE: Treat guidelines for dogs (UC Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital) | https://www.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk491/files/inline-files/treats-guidelines-for-dogs-2020.pdf

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - What fruit can dogs and cats eat?
Fruits a veterinary nutritionist lists as healthy, low calorie treats for dogs and cats are apples, strawberries, blueberries, raspberries, bananas, watermelon, honeydew melon and cantaloupe. Each of these amounts is about 15 calories: a quarter cup of chopped apple, three medium strawberries, 20 blueberries, 15 raspberries, an eighth of a medium banana, or a third of a cup of diced melon.
SOURCE: Treat Options for Dogs and Cats Without Unbalancing Their Diet (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2021/10/21/treat-options-for-dogs-and-cats-without-unbalancing-their-diet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - What vegetables can dogs and cats eat?
Raw vegetables a veterinary nutritionist lists as healthy, low calorie treats for dogs and cats are cucumber, carrots, green beans, sweet red pepper, celery and broccoli. Cooked options are cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, cabbage and broccoli boiled with no salt, and carrots, yellow squash, zucchini and green beans steamed. Four baby carrots, eight green beans or two stalks of celery each come to about 15 calories.
SOURCE: Treat Options for Dogs and Cats Without Unbalancing Their Diet (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2021/10/21/treat-options-for-dogs-and-cats-without-unbalancing-their-diet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Can dogs and cats eat eggs, chicken, pumpkin, yogurt or popcorn?
The same list includes hard boiled egg and boiled or baked chicken or turkey breast. A quarter of a large hard boiled egg is about 20 calories, and a tablespoon of diced chicken or turkey breast is about 15. It also includes canned pumpkin without salt, plain nonfat Greek yogurt and unsalted air popped popcorn, where two tablespoons of pumpkin or yogurt, or half a cup of popcorn, is about 15 calories.
SOURCE: Treat Options for Dogs and Cats Without Unbalancing Their Diet (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2021/10/21/treat-options-for-dogs-and-cats-without-unbalancing-their-diet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Are these treats safe for every pet?
The foods on these lists were chosen as examples of treats that are healthy and relatively low in calories. They may be fine for a healthy pet but not for a pet with a medical condition, so the treats for a pet with any health problem are best agreed with its veterinarian. Weighing a treat gives a more accurate idea of its calories than measuring it by volume.
SOURCE: Treat Options for Dogs and Cats Without Unbalancing Their Diet (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2021/10/21/treat-options-for-dogs-and-cats-without-unbalancing-their-diet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Are bones and rawhides safe?
Bones and rawhides can cause stomach upset or a blockage. They are also high in calories: a palm sized rawhide has about 100 calories and some chew bones have more than 1,000, while an average Labrador retriever needs only about 1,000 calories a day. Crunchy vegetables such as carrot, cucumber, celery and zucchini make good chews instead, and some cats love zucchini as a treat.
SOURCE: What are safe and healthy treats for my pet? (Tufts Petfoodology) | https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2016/01/what-are-safe-and-healthy-treats-for-my-pet/

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Human foods that are toxic to pets
Some foods that are healthy for people are toxic to pets. Garlic and onions can cause blood problems, grapes can cause kidney disease, and chocolate can even be fatal. Too many treats can also harm a pet that has a medical condition.

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Can cats eat human food, raw meat or tuna?
An occasional treat is not generally harmful to a cat, but treats are usually not complete and balanced, so they should stay under 10 to 15 percent of the cat's daily calories. Some foods should be avoided completely. Raw meat is not recommended as a food or a treat for cats because it can carry toxoplasmosis and other infections. Canned fish meant for people, which includes canned tuna, is another: some cats that ate canned fish products meant for humans have developed potentially serious neurological disorders.
SOURCE: Feeding Your Cat (Cornell Feline Health Center) | https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Can cats drink milk?
Milk is not generally recommended as a treat for cats. Many cats are lactose intolerant and can develop stomach and bowel problems if they are fed milk or other dairy products.
SOURCE: Feeding Your Cat (Cornell Feline Health Center) | https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Can dogs and cats have milk, cheese or ice cream?
Pets do not make much lactase, the enzyme that breaks down the sugar in milk, so milk and other dairy foods such as cheese and ice cream can cause diarrhea or other stomach upset in dogs and cats.
SOURCE: People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets (ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center) | https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Is ice cream safe for my dog? Sugar free ice cream and xylitol
Sugar free desserts, including "skinny" ice cream, can be sweetened with xylitol, which is dangerous to dogs, and one of the dog poisoning reports the FDA received was linked to a sugar free ice cream. Check the label for xylitol, especially on anything sold as sugar free or low sugar, and keep any product that contains it out of your dog's reach.
SOURCE: Paws Off Xylitol; It's Dangerous for Dogs (FDA) | https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/paws-xylitol-its-dangerous-dogs

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Is peanut butter safe for my dog?
Some peanut butters and other nut butters contain xylitol, a sweetener that is dangerous to dogs. If you give your dog nut butter as a treat or to hide a pill in, check the label first to make sure it does not contain xylitol.
SOURCE: Paws Off Xylitol; It's Dangerous for Dogs (FDA) | https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/paws-xylitol-its-dangerous-dogs

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - Are eggs safe for my dog or cat? Raw eggs, raw meat and bones
Raw eggs and raw meat can carry bacteria such as Salmonella and E. coli that can harm pets and people alike. Raw eggs also contain an enzyme that can interfere with the absorption of certain vitamins, which can lead to skin and coat problems. Bones can injure or block a dog's or cat's gut, which can be serious and need surgery.
SOURCE: People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets (ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center) | https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets

Human foods and treats for dogs and cats - My dog ate a banana, carrots or other fruit or vegetables. Is that bad?
Bananas, apples, strawberries, blueberries, watermelon, carrots, green beans, cucumber and celery are among the foods a veterinary nutritionist lists as healthy, low calorie treats for dogs and cats. What matters is the amount, since treats should stay under 10 percent of a pet's daily calories, and an eighth of a medium banana or four baby carrots is already about 15 calories. These foods may be fine for a healthy pet but not for one with a medical condition.

Vomiting now and then, such as after eating grass, is likely an isolated incident. Vomiting even once a week, though, may be a sign of disease.
