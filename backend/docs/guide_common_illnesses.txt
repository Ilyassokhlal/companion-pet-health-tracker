Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Cataracts in dogs, a cloudy eye and failing sight
A cataract is a lens in the eye that has turned cloudy or opaque, which blocks light from reaching the retina and causes anything from mild vision problems to blindness. It is not the same as the normal thickening and hardening of the lens that comes with age. Cataracts are often inherited in dogs, and other causes include diabetes, malnutrition, radiation, inflammation and injury. Because dogs rely on hearing and smell, early cataracts are hard for owners to notice. A dog that is losing its sight may move more cautiously and stay closer to its owner than usual.
SOURCE: Disorders of the Lens in Dogs (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/eye-disorders-of-dogs/disorders-of-the-lens-in-dogs

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - How cataracts in dogs are treated
Treatment usually means surgery to remove the affected lens, and it is most likely to succeed before the cataract has fully matured and before leaking lens material inflames the front of the eye. Complications are possible. A dog that does not have surgery still needs regular checks, because complications such as glaucoma can develop. Cataracts present at birth may shrink as the lens grows, and immature cataracts may benefit from eye drops.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Heart murmur and mitral valve disease in older small dogs
Degenerative valve disease is the most common heart disease in dogs, about 75 percent of their heart disease. The heart valves thicken, most often the mitral or tricuspid valve, so they no longer close properly. It is more common in older small breed dogs and is believed to be inherited in Cavalier King Charles Spaniels and Dachshunds. In the early stages there are no signs apart from a heart murmur the veterinarian can hear, and only about 30 percent of dogs with a leaking mitral valve go on to heart failure. When it becomes severe, a dog may be unwilling to move or exercise, have difficulty breathing, cough or faint.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Mitral valve disease, counting breaths and treatment
It is diagnosed from the physical examination with chest x rays and an ultrasound of the heart. A veterinarian may ask the owner to count the dog's breaths per minute while it sleeps, because a raised sleeping breathing rate can mean fluid is building up in the lungs, and regular counts show how the dog responds to treatment. Treatment in small breed dogs starts when signs of heart failure appear or fluid is seen in the lungs, and usually includes an ACE inhibitor, a diuretic and pimobendan. Survival times vary, but treated dogs can live for more than a year.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Stomatitis in cats, a painful mouth
Feline stomatitis is a deep, worsening inflammation of the mouth, gums and upper throat. The first sign is severe pain on opening the mouth: the cat may cry out or jump when it yawns or picks up food, and it may approach its food hungrily and then hiss and run off. It may also have bad breath, heavy drooling and difficulty swallowing, and a cat with severe, long standing disease may become noticeably thinner. The cause is unproven, but it may be an inappropriate inflammatory response to something on the tooth surface, and many affected cats carry calicivirus long term.
SOURCE: Disorders of the Mouth in Cats (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/cat-owners/digestive-disorders-of-cats/disorders-of-the-mouth-in-cats

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - How stomatitis in cats is treated
Removing all the premolars and molars, and sometimes every tooth, is the only treatment that has brought improvement and helped with long term control. Done early in the disease, it brings major improvement or complete resolution in 60 to 80 percent of cats, but the response is poor when extraction is delayed. Cats eat well without their teeth. Antibiotics are given when there is a bacterial infection.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Constipation in cats
Constipation, passing feces rarely or with difficulty, is a common problem in cats and is usually easily corrected. The most common cause is hair, bones or litter mixed into the feces. Not drinking enough and holding on because of a dirty litter box, another cat guarding the box or pain can also dry the feces out, and some drugs cause constipation. Signs are straining and firm, dry feces, and some cats are also lethargic, off their food, vomiting or uncomfortable in the belly, so a visit to the veterinarian is advised.
SOURCE: Disorders of the Stomach and Intestines in Cats (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/cat-owners/digestive-disorders-of-cats/disorders-of-the-stomach-and-intestines-in-cats

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Treating constipation and megacolon in cats
A constipated cat should have plenty of water. Mild cases often respond to a high fiber diet, keeping the cat away from bones and other objects, more litter boxes cleaned more often, and a short course of a laxative prescribed by the veterinarian. Laxatives made for people can be very dangerous for pets. In severe cases the veterinarian removes the retained feces with enemas or by hand under general anesthesia. Some cats with long term constipation develop megacolon, an enlarged colon whose muscle has weakened, and cats that do not respond to medical treatment may need the affected part of the large intestine removed.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Bladder infection (UTI) in dogs
Signs of a bacterial bladder infection include urinating often, painful or difficult urination, urinating in the wrong places and blood in the urine, often most noticeable at the end of the stream. Some dogs show no signs, especially those on long term steroids or with Cushing's disease, diabetes or kidney disease. A urine sample is tested to diagnose it, and simple infections are treated with antibiotics by mouth for 2 weeks, or longer if needed, with urine rechecked during and after treatment. Proper treatment matters because the bacteria can become resistant, the infection can spread to the kidneys, and untreated infections are a common cause of struvite stones. Most bacterial infections of dogs cannot be passed to people.
SOURCE: Infectious Diseases of the Urinary System in Dogs (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/kidney-and-urinary-tract-disorders-of-dogs/infectious-diseases-of-the-urinary-system-in-dogs

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Bladder infection (UTI) in cats
Healthy adult cats rarely get bacterial bladder infections. Elderly cats, cats with a weak immune system such as those with feline leukemia virus or feline immunodeficiency virus, cats on long term steroids and cats with diabetes or thyroid disease are more prone. The signs are the same as in dogs: urinating often, painful or difficult urination, urinating in the wrong places and blood in the urine. It is diagnosed from a urine sample and treated with antibiotics. An untreated infection can spread to the kidneys, and infections that keep coming back can point to an underlying problem.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Lyme disease in dogs
Lyme disease is caused by bacteria spread by infected ticks, mostly the deer or black legged tick, which usually has to stay attached for 1 to 2 days to pass it on. Spring and autumn are the riskiest times. Many infected dogs show no signs. When they do, the signs include fever, loss of appetite, swollen or painful joints, lameness that shifts from one leg to another, swollen lymph nodes and lack of energy. Untreated, it can damage the kidneys, nervous system and heart, and the kidney form is one of the most serious and is often fatal.
SOURCE: Lyme Disease in Dogs (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/disorders-affecting-multiple-body-systems-of-dogs/lyme-disease-lyme-borreliosis-in-dogs

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Treating and preventing Lyme disease in dogs
Treatment is usually antibiotics for about 4 weeks. Most dogs with joint or leg signs improve quickly, though some develop long term joint pain. Prevention relies on using a tick control product consistently, vaccination where Lyme disease is common, and checking dogs for ticks after time outdoors. Remove a tick with fine tipped tweezers by gripping its head close to the skin and pulling straight out, without squeezing its body. Dogs do not pass Lyme disease directly to people, but they can bring infected ticks into the home.
SOURCE: Lyme Disease in Dogs (Merck Veterinary Manual) | https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/disorders-affecting-multiple-body-systems-of-dogs/lyme-disease-lyme-borreliosis-in-dogs

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Anaplasmosis in dogs
Anaplasma bacteria are spread by ticks such as the brown dog tick, lone star tick and black legged tick, and are found in many parts of the world. Anaplasma phagocytophilum causes anaplasmosis in people and can also make dogs sick. Anaplasma platys causes infectious cyclic thrombocytopenia, repeated drops in the platelets that help blood clot, although infected dogs usually show few or no signs. These infections are usually treated with a course of antibiotics, and tick control is the most important way to prevent them.

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Vestibular syndrome in old dogs, head tilt and loss of balance
Vestibular syndrome is a sudden balance problem that is often mistaken for dementia. Without warning the dog walks in circles with its head tilted, or cannot stand at all. It acts nauseated when fed and has trouble eating and drinking, and its eyes flick rapidly sideways, up and down or in circles. No cause can be found. The signs usually improve after the first day or so, and most dogs return to normal, although it can take weeks and the head tilt may stay, which dogs adapt to well. There is no established treatment, but a veterinarian may prescribe medicine to help the dog eat and drink. A support harness or a towel under the belly helps it walk. Keeping it up and moving prevents pressure sores, and it needs a well padded bed when lying down.
SOURCE: Senior dog dementia (Cornell Riney Canine Health Center) | https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/senior-dog-dementia

Common illnesses in dogs and cats - Vestibular syndrome in cats, head tilt and falling over
A cat with vestibular disease suddenly circles or falls to one side, holds its head tilted and has rapid, involuntary eye movements. Causes range from bacterial infections, inflammation and reactions to some drugs to polyps and tumors, so a veterinarian should see the cat straight away. In most cases no cause is found. This idiopathic form improves over several days and most cats recover completely within two or three weeks. It has no specific treatment: the cat is kept confined somewhere safe, helped to eat and drink if needed, and given anti nausea medicine if it vomits.
