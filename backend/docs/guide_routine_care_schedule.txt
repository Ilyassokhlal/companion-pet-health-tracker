Routine care schedule for dogs and cats
Routine care is the regular care an animal needs to stay healthy: veterinary checkups, vaccinations, parasite control, dental care, good food, grooming, and a home without hazards in reach.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - How often a dog sees the vet
An adult dog needs a full checkup at least once a year. Puppies are seen every three to four weeks until they are four months old. Dogs over about seven or eight years old should go twice a year or more, because older dogs fall ill more often, and the veterinarian may suggest blood tests or X rays to catch problems early.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - How often a cat sees the vet
An adult cat needs a full checkup at least once a year. Kittens are seen every three to four weeks until they are about six months old. Cats over about eight or nine years old should go twice a year or more, often with regular blood tests to look for early signs of chronic illness. A cat that seems unwell should be taken in straight away rather than waiting for the next checkup.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - What a checkup is for
A checkup is a full physical examination, and in older animals it is often paired with blood tests and sometimes X rays. For a geriatric cat, one option is a yearly examination with blood work and X rays plus a shorter check halfway through the year, and checks every six months suit many older cats.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Vaccinations for dogs
Core vaccines, recommended by nearly all veterinarians, protect against distemper, parvovirus and rabies. Other vaccines, such as those for Lyme disease or Bordetella, matter in particular areas or lifestyles. Boosters continue through life: some are needed every few years, others yearly, and the veterinarian sets the schedule from where you live and what the dog does.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Vaccinations for cats
Vaccination matters for every cat, indoors or out. The American Animal Hospital Association and the Feline Veterinary Medical Association publish the guidelines veterinarians follow, and the choice depends on the area and the cat's lifestyle. Boosters may be needed through the cat's life. Mild soreness or a slight fever after a vaccination is normal, serious allergic reactions are rare, and tumours at the injection site are very rare.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Parasite control
A dog's stool is tested once a year to find and treat intestinal parasites. Roundworms and hookworms can also infect people, usually through contact with contaminated ground, so picking up stools and washing hands protects the household as well as the dog.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Parasite control
Heartworm is spread by mosquito bites and lives in the heart and bloodstream. Dogs should be tested for heartworm every year and given preventive medicine all year round, and many heartworm products also cover intestinal worms. Ask the veterinarian which flea, tick and worm preventives suit your animal and your area.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Dental care at home
Brushing is the core of dental care for both dogs and cats, using a toothbrush and toothpaste made for animals, alongside dental toys and professional cleanings when the veterinarian advises them. Regular brushing slows the plaque and tartar that lead to gum disease and tooth loss, exactly as it does in people.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Grooming
Brush a dog regularly to remove loose hair and stop mats forming, which matters most for thick or long coats, because matted hair irritates the skin and can lead to infection. Never cut mats out with scissors, since it is easy to cut the skin. Use clippers made for pets. Bathing about once a month suits most dogs, adjusted for the season and for how dirty the coat gets.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Spaying and neutering
Unless an animal is being bred, it should be spayed or neutered. Female dogs are usually spayed at around six months, before the first heat, which helps prevent mammary and uterine cancer. Male dogs are usually neutered between five and ten months, which can reduce urine marking and other behaviour problems.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - A safe home
Keep cleaning products, antifreeze and toxic plants where an animal cannot reach them. Puppies chew, so they are most at risk, and electrical cords should be tied out of reach or treated with a spray that tastes bitter.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - Cleaning and hygiene
Clean food and water bowls, scoops, feeding mats and treat toys often. Pet bowls are cleaned after every meal of wet food, and daily for dry food and water. Litter boxes and anything else that touches urine or faeces should be disinfected as often as you would disinfect a toilet, and more often if the animal is ill.

Routine care schedule for dogs and cats - What a yearly wellness examination covers
An annual wellness examination is a full physical check by a veterinarian: they listen to the heart and lungs, feel the abdomen, and examine the limbs, along with the eyes, ears, mouth and skin, and they will ask about appetite, drinking, toilet habits, activity and behaviour at home. It is also when vaccinations are reviewed and boosted, a stool sample is checked for worms, the annual heartworm test is done for dogs, weight and body condition are assessed, and dental care is discussed. In older animals the visit often adds blood tests, and sometimes urine tests or X rays, to catch chronic disease before it shows.
